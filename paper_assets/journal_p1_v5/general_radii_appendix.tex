\section{Coverage and strictness for prescribed integer radii}\label{app:general}
Fix $n\ge3$ distinct positive integers $a_1<\cdots<a_n$, rational
endpoints $0<L<U$, and an integer scale $S=2^b$. The general-radii
verifier establishes symmetric integer weights $w_{ij}$ and an integer
threshold $T$ satisfying
\[
  0\le w_{ij}/S\le\theta_L(a_i,a_j),\qquad 2\pi<T/S.
\]
It encloses $2\pi=32\arctan(1/5)-8\arctan(1/239)$ by rational alternating
sums. For each positive $w_{ij}$ it checks $w_{ij}/S<\pi$ and uses an
outward-rounded cosine series to prove
$\cos(w_{ij}/S)>1-2a_i a_j/((L+a_i)(L+a_j))$. Monotonicity of cosine
on $[0,\pi]$ gives the angular bound. A zero weight is valid directly.

\paragraph{Lifting a retained cycle.}
Delete the $d$ smallest radii, where $0\le d\le n-3$, and put $k=n-d$.
Represent each cyclic order of the $k$ retained labels by anchoring its
largest label and choosing the orientation in which the successor is
smaller than the predecessor. These neighbors are distinct because
$k\ge3$. Insert the deleted labels in a fixed sequence. With $s$ labels
present there are $s$ choices of gap, including the closing gap. Removing
the inserted labels in reverse sequence recovers every choice. Conversely,
a full unoriented cyclic order inducing this core has exactly one
orientation that restricts to the chosen core orientation, even if an
insertion changes an anchor neighbor. Hence the number of full classes
above each core is
\[
 I_{n,k}=\prod_{s=k}^{n-1}s=\frac{(n-1)!}{(k-1)!}.
\]
For $d=0$ this is one. The verifier enumerates the lifts independently of
sequential insertion. It chooses a weakly increasing list of $d$ core-gap
indices and a permutation of the deleted labels, then fills the designated
gaps in that order. Reading each gap in turn recovers both choices.

\paragraph{Partition of the full orders.}
Apply recurrence~\eqref{eq:dp} to the retained labels with anchor $a_n$.
Reversing the completion path gives the least possible total integer
cycle cost for a prefix, as in~\eqref{eq:prune}. Prune only when that
minimum exceeds $T$. The induced-cycle inequality~\eqref{eq:induced}
then excludes every lift of every completion. A pruned prefix with $h$
remaining labels accounts for $h!$ oriented cores. Let $P$ sum these
counts over the disjoint pruned prefixes, and let $K$ count the surviving
canonical cores. Survival is invariant under reversal since the complete
cycle cost is symmetric. Each core has two distinct orientations, so $P$
is even. With $E$ explicit full orders the partition satisfies
\[
 P+2K=(k-1)!,\qquad E=K I_{n,k},\qquad
 \frac{P}{2}I_{n,k}+E=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}.
\]
The verifier recomputes the traversal and checks every lift of every
survivor. It first tests induced cycles obtained by deleting initial
segments of the ranked radii, including the empty segment. These tests
exclude all explicit orders in the two reported families.

For a general input there is also a difference-constraint test. Write
$x_i$ for the unwrapped angles in word order, scaled by $S$, and write
$w_{ij}$ for the weight of the labels at positions $i,j$. By
\eqref{eq:difference}, feasibility implies
$x_j\ge x_i+w_{ij}$ and $x_i\ge x_j+w_{ij}-T$ for $i<j$.
A directed cycle of positive integer weight contradicts these inequalities
by summation. The verifier tests for such a cycle by maximizing path
weights in the Floyd--Warshall recurrence. A positive closed walk contains
a positive simple cycle, by decomposition at repeated vertices. An order
for which neither test gives a violation leaves the lower audit incomplete.

\paragraph{A rational buffer above the lower endpoint.}
An excluded induced cycle has integer weight at least $T+1$, hence true
angular excess at least $1/S$ at $L$. A positive simple constraint cycle
with $h$ backward edges similarly gives
$\sum\theta_L(a_i,a_j)-h\,2\pi\ge1/S$. Each of these cycles has at most
$n$ edges. For $R\ge L$, differentiating~\eqref{eq:kernel} gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\partial\theta_R(a,b)}{\partial R}\right|
 =\sqrt{\frac{ab}{R(R+a+b)}}\left(\frac1{R+a}+\frac1{R+b}\right)
 \le\frac{2a_n}{L^2}.
\]
Indeed the square root is at most $a_n/L$ and the sum is at most $2/L$.
The excess in each necessary inequality can therefore decrease by at most
$D\delta$ between $L$ and $L+\delta$, where $D=2na_n/L^2$. Set
\[
 \varepsilon=\min\{(U-L)/2,\ 1/(2SD)\}>0.
\]
Every violation retains at least $1/(2S)$ throughout $[L,L+\varepsilon]$.
For smaller positive radii the angles only increase. Complete lower
coverage thus places the global infimum at or above $L+\varepsilon>L$.
At $U$, the verifier checks the claimed order, central tangencies and
all outer-pair separations of the rational centers in~\eqref{eq:stereo},
using the prescribed radii. This proves the closed upper endpoint.
