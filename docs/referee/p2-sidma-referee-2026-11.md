# Referee report on *The minimum central circle for prescribed radius profiles*

**SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics · November 2026 · Internal adversarial review**

**Recommendation: major revision before submission.** The radius-profile law (Theorem 5.3) holds as stated. I find no missing hypothesis in the limit or word-LP theorems. A fresh verifier also recovers both endpoints for consecutive radii and the strict square-radius ceiling. My reservation concerns how much new discrete mathematics a reader can identify after separating the law theorem from the earlier uniform case and from the finite shelf problem.

## Summary and contribution

For disks of radii $N_n a_{n,i}$ around a central circle, with $N_n>0$ and $0\le a_{n,i}\le M<\infty$ uniformly in $n$, the author identifies the leading coefficient of the least feasible central radius. It depends only on the weak limit of the empirical mark distribution. The key geometric move is to cut and close an auxiliary circle with geometric-mean separation. The cost of closing is bounded independently of the number and order of the marks. A sorted matching argument then transfers line templates to all bounded empirical laws, including laws with atoms at zero.

The uniform law and its finite-word characterization were already treated in the author's sequel. For a general law, the substantial additions are stability under distributional perturbations, recovery along every array with the same law, and an additive finite-word bracket. There is also a useful change of upper-bound construction: one certified finite line placement suffices. For the uniform case the resulting interval has width $0.000389107258$. S1's optimized deletion argument accounts for the lower endpoint. The finite coordinates for the upper endpoint give an existence witness. Their search history is heuristic.

## Major issues

1. **Identify the mathematical advance over shelf packing and over v3.** The fixed-order recurrence and the shelf geometry have close antecedents. Alt et al. give a $4/3$ approximation, an exact finite algorithm in a class including radius ratio below four, and finite NP-hardness even at bounded ratio. For marks at most $M$, the shelf envelope exceeds twice the line span by at most $2M$; the two minimization problems therefore have the same leading mean cost up to the factor of two. A reader needs to see where those finite results stop and the law theorem begins. The listed NP-hardness result supplies no hardness statement for evaluating $E(\mu)$. Distinguish the results already present in v3 in the introduction. [Alt et al., JoCG](https://jocg.org/index.php/jocg/article/view/3056) · [Earlier asymptotic paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13630v3)
2. **Put the square-radius example against its strongest documented comparator.** The paper gives $1/(6\pi)\le C_2<0.088581212993$. The pinned quadratic project already gives a lower liminf bound $(434+4\sqrt 2)/(1587\pi)$ and an upper limsup bound $857/(3000\pi)$. Their approximate values are 0.08818344249 and 0.09093052415, while $1/(6\pi)$ is about 0.05305165. Thus the law theorem settles convergence, and the new witness improves the prior upper side. The elementary lower bound illustrates the general theorem but conceals the previously available precision. This comparison is an attribution to the indicated proof, whose full chain is outside this independent replay. [Pinned quadratic proof, bounds (22)–(23)](https://github.com/falker47/ringmin-squared/blob/1d0a5dea10cd09783a9c0e2d54ede79b790cce2b/research/ALL_N_LOWER_BOUND.md)
3. **Make the force and cost of the LP guarantee intelligible.** Its balance constraints couple label counts only in expectation. The reason it bounds the physical problem is that a rational minimizing vertex permits an integer batch, with at most $M$ paid per join. This is a substantive recovery argument. The additive guarantee holds before word-cost evaluation and entails $k^r$ words in the displayed formulation. Already $k=r=64$ means $64^{64}$ words for width just under 0.01 at $M=1$. For arbitrary real laws even a one-point quantile can be noncomputable unless the input representation supplies it. A small nontrivial example with an explicit primal/dual pair, or a distribution for which the bracket can be interpreted without enumeration, would make the computational contribution easier to judge.
4. **Give the law theorem priority in the proof architecture.** The uniform squeeze, replication and finite-word proofs occupy Sections 2–4 before their general counterparts in Section 5. The two replication arguments use different deletions and the same underlying closing principle. One derivation of the general law followed by specializations would expose what the generalization buys. In particular, monotonicity appears only in the quantile template. The original labels may be arbitrarily permuted and their sampling profile may be nonmonotone. A simple atomic example and a failing grid sample would make this visible.
5. **Separate three kinds of error in the results and interpretation.** There is an explicit angular error $O(M/n)$, a structural LP width $M(1/k+1/r)/\pi$, and a sampling error governed by the distance of the empirical law to its limit. Weak convergence supplies no uniform rate for the third. The two endpoint witnesses bound limiting coefficients; the twelve plotted finite brackets need not be in that band. The square-root matching estimate gives the reader a way to assess a specified family. State the scope of any desired finite-size rates and reserve finite optimality, sharpness and optimizer structure for the objects for which they were proved.

## Independent derivation of the profile result

Let $b(a)$ be the least line span subject to $|x_i-x_j|\ge\sqrt{a_i a_j}$ for all pairs. In each order it is the longest forward path cost, since each right-hand coordinate must exceed every previous coordinate by the relevant edge weight. Taking the minimum over finitely many orders attains $b$.

**Geometry.** For $R>0$ the cosine law bounds each required angle between $2\sqrt{uv}/(R+N_n M)$ and $2\sqrt{uv}/R$. Scaling and cutting a feasible ring gives the lower side of (5.1); closing an optimal line with a gap $M$ makes both complementary arcs feasible and gives the upper side. Consequently
```text
b(a_n)/(πn) − M/n ≤ R_n/(N_n n) ≤ b(a_n)/(πn) + M/(πn).
```
This works with coincident zero marks under the paper's point convention and gives zero directly at $M=0$.

**Replication.** Write $Q$ for the nondecreasing quantile of the candidate law. Fix $n$, concatenate $q=\lceil m/n\rceil$ copies with gap $M$ and drop the $d=nq-m$ smallest donors. Donor $i$ of the retained sorted list has argument $\lceil(i+d)/q\rceil/n\ge i/m$ because $m(i+d)-nqi=d(m-i)\ge0$. Decreasing it to $Q(i/m)$ preserves feasibility even at ties. Dividing the span by $m$ and then taking the infimum over $n$ identifies $E(\mu)=\inf_{n\ge2}(b_n^Q+M)/n$ with the ordinary limit of $b_m^Q/m$. Every integer $m$ enters this argument.

**Stability and sampling.** On any forward path each vertex is an endpoint at most twice. Changing matched marks therefore changes the minimum span by at most $2\sqrt M\sum_i|\sqrt{a_i}-\sqrt{c_i}|$, uniformly in the order. For $c_i=Q(i/n)$ the root-mark CDF at $s$ equals $\lfloor nF_\mu(s^2)\rfloor/n$. Monotone matching identifies the average root-mark distance with the integral of the absolute difference of the two CDFs. Weak convergence gives convergence at almost every threshold and $[0,\sqrt M]$ has finite length. Thus that distance vanishes. The squeeze proves $R_n/(N_n n)\to E(\mu)/\pi$ for every $N_n>0$. No derivative of a sampled function enters.

For a bounded measurable $f$ the exact condition identifying the intended *sampling law* is convergence of its grid averages on all continuous tests to the integral against $f_*\mathrm{Leb}$. Equivalently, test the proportions with $f(i/n)\le s$ at continuity thresholds of that law. Riemann integrability is a convenient sufficient condition. Weak convergence to some other law still gives the array theorem with that other law. Equality of a single limiting coefficient is weaker than identification of a law, so there is no necessity claim for this scalar alone.

| Stress case | Scope and proof or counterexample |
|---|---|
| Nonmonotone tent $f(x)=1-|2x-1|$ | Applies. The two preimages of almost every height have equal inverse slope $1/2$. Its pushforward is uniform, so it has exactly the consecutive-radius coefficient for $N_n=n$. |
| Vanishing, including all-zero profile | Applies. Changing $f(0)$ affects no right-endpoint sample and no pushforward. At $f\equiv0$ both spans and infimal radii vanish. No positive lower bound on individual marks is needed. |
| Jump and zero atom, $c\mathbf1_{[1/2,1]}$, $c>0$ | Applies. With $k=n-\lceil n/2\rceil+1$ positive marks, $b=c(k-1)$ for $k\ge1$: equally space the positive marks and place the zeros anywhere. The normalized ring coefficient is $c/(2\pi)$. |
| $f=x^p$, $p=1/100$ or $100$ | Applies for either fixed exponent. Rearrangement gives $E\ge B(1+p/2,1+p/2)>0$. Consecutive gaps $(a_i+a_{i+1})/2$ suffice for all pairs by AM–GM, giving $E\le1/(p+1)$. The lower evaluations are $B(201/200,201/200)$ and $(50!)^2/101!$; the upper bounds are $100/101$ and $1/101$. For $n f(i/n)$ divide by $n^2$; for actual radii $i^p$ take $N_n=n^p$ and divide by $n^{p+1}$. The corollary asserts a fixed-$p$ limit. |
| Nowhere Riemann-integrable but correctly sampled | The profile clause is silent. Take the indicator of a countable dense set of irrationals. The samples and $f(U)$ all vanish almost surely (every grid value is exactly zero), so the array theorem applies. |
| Measurable $f=\mathbf1_{\mathbb Q}$ | The unsupplemented pushforward assertion fails: all grid marks equal one but $f(U)=0$ almost surely. Equally spaced centers give $R_n/N_n=\csc(\pi/n)-1$ by summing adjacent angles. The actual coefficient is $1/\pi$. |
| Indicator of rationals having even reduced denominator | The array hypothesis fails. Along odd $n$ all marks vanish, while for $n=2^j$ there are $n-1$ unit marks. The normalized radius has limits $0$ and $1/\pi$ along those subsequences. |
| Weakly convergent array with unbounded marks | Outside the theorem. Take three marks $n^2$ and $n-3$ zeros. Their empirical laws tend weakly to $\delta_0$, but three equal disks require $R_n/(N_n n)\ge n(2/\sqrt3-1)$. Weak convergence alone cannot replace the common bound. |

All these tests are exact examples or deductions. They do not change the paper's theorem. All marks $\varepsilon_n\downarrow0$ give $b(a_n)/n=(1-1/n)\varepsilon_n$ and illustrate arbitrarily slow sampling convergence.

## SIDMA go/no-go

**No-go for submission of this version; major revision with SIDMA retained as a target.** The generalization passes its mathematical gate. A law-stable limit and a recoverable finite-word variational bound are plausible contributions to discrete optimization. In this draft, duplicate uniform proofs, the missing quadratic comparison and the absence of a worked nonuniform LP example make their significance harder to judge than their correctness. SIDMA explicitly seeks significant theoretical contributions in discrete mathematics, including optimization and discrete geometry. Beiträge is a credible alternative if the author prefers to foreground geometric inequalities, witnesses and their proofs. This is an editorial recommendation; neither coverage by a journal's scope nor internal verification predicts acceptance.
